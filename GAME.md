# Puzz

A puzzle adventure in a house full of puzzles, in the tradition of *The 7th
Guest*, *The 11th Hour* and *The 13th Doll* (see `inspiration/`), but **bright
and light-hearted instead of scary**: a sunny, toy-like, cel-shaded house
(the look of Schnau), warm colours, playful characters, and humour in place
of dread. The house, characters, story and puzzles are Puzz's own.

## The idea: the holidays are all mixed up

The holidays live in a house: each one has its own room, a little world
done up for its day. And someone has **mixed them all up**. There's an
Easter egg in the Christmas tree, a firework stuck in the Thanksgiving
centrepiece, Cupid's bow strung up in the Fourth of July fireworks rack.

Every **stray item** is caught up in a puzzle, in the style of the holiday
it landed in: the egg is one of the tree's ornaments, so freeing it means
solving the ornament puzzle. **Beat the puzzle, and the item is yours.**
Carry it home to its own holiday. Return everything a holiday is missing,
and it comes back to life: lights, music, its centrepiece puzzle, and a
page of the calendar.

You choose the order. Every holiday's door is open, every stray is there to
find, so there's always something else to try when a puzzle has you stuck
(as in *Safecracker*, where each safe hands you keys and clues for others).

## The host: Ordinary Tuesday

**Ordinary Tuesday** is the most ordinary day of the year. Nobody throws a
party for a Tuesday, nobody sends Tuesday cards, and Tuesday has had enough.
So Tuesday mixed up all the holidays, to see how *they* like it.

Tuesday is the house's host, the way Stauf hosts *The 7th Guest*, but a
villain with a soft centre: beige, a bit sulky, very funny about it. Tuesday
pops up to grumble when you fix things, leaves notes on puzzles, rigs the
odd trick, and slowly warms to you. The hall that joins the holidays is
Tuesday's: plain, beige, utterly ordinary, the joke against all that colour.

**The ending:** with all six holidays restored and their six calendar pages
gathered, the pages make the final puzzle: putting together a holiday for
Tuesday, with a tradition borrowed from each of the others. Tuesday gets a
party at last.

## Getting around

- **Each holiday is one diorama, seen in a single fixed view**: the whole
  room laid out like a toy theatre set, everything in it on show. No
  walking about inside a holiday.
- **Tuesday's hall is the hub**, with a door to each holiday. Click a door
  to go into that holiday; click to go back to the hall.
- So any holiday is two clicks from any other: the hall in between.
- Within a diorama you click things: a stray's puzzle, the centrepiece, the
  spot where a returned item belongs (and later, the gags). A puzzle comes up
  close to play, then drops back to the diorama.

## The six holidays

Each is a room in the house, a diorama of its day with its own palette and
music, and its own puzzle style (which every stray item caught there uses).

| Holiday | The room | Its puzzle style |
|---|---|---|
| **Valentine's Day** | A pink and red sweetheart parlour: lace, roses, a heart-shaped sofa. | Logic and words: sorting a box of chocolates by clues, love letters with scrambled words, bouncing Cupid's arrows off mirrors. |
| **Easter** | A spring garden: pastel eggs, tulips, a hutch, a basket-weaver's bench. | Pattern and colour: dyeing eggs by mixing colours, weaving over and under, the bunny's hops across the garden. |
| **Fourth of July** | A backyard cookout at dusk: picnic tables, a grill, bunting, a fireworks rack. | Timing and order: firework shows set off in the right sequence, lining up a parade, the grill's timings. |
| **Halloween** | A cosy pumpkin patch at night: friendly ghosts, a porch, a candy bowl. (Spooky-cute, never scary.) | Shape and swaps: carving a pumpkin to a silhouette, sorting out mixed-up costumes, trading candy. |
| **Thanksgiving** | A harvest dining room: the long table, a hearth, pies cooling. | Measure and fairness: seating the family by everyone's wishes, balancing a recipe on the scales, slicing a pie fairly. |
| **Christmas** | A snowy living room: the tree, stockings, a toy workshop corner. | Packing and pattern: wrapping presents (fitting shapes in a box), symmetrical ornaments, the workshop's gears. |

(New Year's, St. Patrick's Day and others can join later.)

## The stray items

Each holiday is missing two things, and two strays from other holidays are
caught in its room. The first map:

| Item | Its home | Found in | Its puzzle there |
|---|---|---|---|
| Golden Egg | Easter | Valentine's | Hidden in a box of chocolates: work out which chocolate is which from the clues on the lid. |
| Pumpkin Pie | Thanksgiving | Valentine's | Heart-shaped, at the end of Cupid's arrow: aim the arrow off the mirrors to reach it. |
| Star-Spangled Flag | Fourth of July | Easter | Woven through the bunny's basket: re-weave the strands to the pattern. |
| Tree-Top Star | Christmas | Easter | Painted on an egg: mix the dyes to match the colours. |
| Cupid's Bow | Valentine's | Fourth of July | Strung up as a firework launcher: set off the show in the right order. |
| Witch's Broom | Halloween | Fourth of July | Carried in the parade: line the parade up as the program says. |
| Box of Chocolates | Valentine's | Halloween | Mixed into the trick-or-treat candy: trade your way to it. |
| Cornucopia | Thanksgiving | Halloween | Someone's wearing it as a costume: work out who's dressed as what. |
| Firework Rocket | Fourth of July | Thanksgiving | The table's centrepiece: seat everyone so it can be lifted out. |
| Stocking | Christmas | Thanksgiving | Weighed down on the kitchen scales: balance the recipe to free it. |
| Bunny's Basket | Easter | Christmas | Wrapped as a present: pack the gift box so it closes. |
| Jack-o'-Lantern | Halloween | Christmas | Hung on the tree as an ornament: make the tree symmetrical. |

Then each restored holiday opens its **centrepiece puzzle** (the biggest in
the room), which earns its calendar page.

## Scope: 19 puzzles

The game is **19 puzzles**: 12 strays (two in each holiday), 6 centrepieces
(one per holiday) and Tuesday's final puzzle. That's the scale of *The 7th
Guest* (~21) and *The 11th Hour* (~20 and its board games); their puzzles
are spread about one per room, ours three per holiday, since a holiday is a
whole diorama rather than a single room.

### Room left for a lighter layer (later)

Between the big puzzles, the inspirations have a lighter layer: *11th Hour*'s
~49 riddles (which object is meant, and where is it?), *Shivers*' pots and
lids to pair up, clickable scenery and story scenes. Puzz will have one too,
filled in after the puzzles:

- **Finding the strays:** a teasing note from Tuesday hints where a stray is
  hiding ("Something round and golden is keeping very sweet company").
  Spotting it is the quick win; its puzzle is the big one.
- **Gags:** clickable jokes and reactions all over each room, above all
  round the mash-ups.

Until then strays sit in plain sight and rooms have only their puzzles, but
the game is built so this layer slots in: each room has hotspots for things
to look at and react, a stray can start hidden until found, and Tuesday's
notes have somewhere to live.

## Design principles

- **Every puzzle is fair and explained.** Its rules can be worked out from
  the room (or Tuesday's notes), and it can always be reset. The UHS hint
  files in `inspiration/` show how good puzzles are meant to be understood.
- **Never stuck.** Several things to try at any moment. Hints are always
  available, gently graded from a nudge to the answer.
- **Mixed-up is funny.** Every stray item is a visual joke in its new room.
  Lean into the mash-ups.
- **Cosy, not scary**, even at Halloween.
